SERIES 02 · WINTER CELEBRATIONS

Nia

Home-made cards, family time and a new idea

📖 Read Nia's story

I love December in Cardiff. The streets are full of lights, and there are so many colours to draw. I am Nia, I am thirteen, and I live with Mum and Gran. In our house, Christmas is more about spending time together than buying lots of things.

Before Christmas, I make cards for people instead of buying them. I draw little winter pictures and sometimes add a funny cartoon. Gran keeps every card I give her. She says Christmas Day, on 25 December, is a Christian celebration of the birth of Jesus, although many people also enjoy it as a family holiday.

On Christmas morning, Mum makes breakfast while Gran opens my card. She always looks at it carefully before she opens a present. Later, we have a long lunch, listen to music and play a word game. The three of us are terrible at agreeing on the rules, but that is half the fun.

The next day is Boxing Day, on 26 December. I used to think it had something to do with the sport. Gran explained that one possible reason for the name is that people once gave boxes of gifts or money to workers. We usually have a slow day. This year, I sort through my drawing things and put some unused pencils aside to donate later. Then we go for a walk if the weather is dry.

On New Year's Eve, I watch the countdown with Mum and Gran. When the clock reaches midnight, we wish each other a happy new year. New Year's Day is 1 January. I like starting it with a blank page in my sketchbook.

My plan for the new year is to finish a short animation. Sharing my work still makes me nervous, so I will show it to one friend first. That seems like a good first step. Christmas gives me time with my family, Boxing Day gives me a chance to slow down, and New Year's Day makes me think about what comes next.

SERIES 02 · WINTER CELEBRATIONS

Evie

Stage nerves, family laughter and a brave plan

📖 Read Evie's story

I am Evie, I am fourteen, and I live in Newcastle upon Tyne with Dad, my stepmum and my younger stepbrother. December is one of my favourite months because there is music everywhere. I go to a youth theatre group, so it is also a busy time for me.

A few days before Christmas, our theatre group puts on a winter show. I have a short singing part. I love rehearsals, but just before I walk on stage, my hands feel cold and my heart beats fast. Then the music begins, I see my family smiling, and I remember why I enjoy it.

Christmas Day is on 25 December. My little stepbrother gets up ridiculously early. He puts a paper star on top of our tree and wants to open every present immediately. We make him wait until everyone is awake. After breakfast, Dad cooks dinner and I help my stepmum set the table. I try to make everyone laugh, especially when my stepbrother starts pretending he is the director of my theatre show.

Boxing Day is the next day, 26 December. The name sounds strange, but it is not a holiday about the sport of boxing. At school, we learnt that people once gave Christmas boxes with money or gifts to workers. We normally go for a long walk and eat the food left from Christmas dinner. I like that we do not have to hurry anywhere.

On New Year's Eve, we stay at home and watch the countdown. We cheer at midnight. The following morning, on New Year's Day, we have a late breakfast. I write a short list of things I want to try this year.

At the top of the list is a bigger part in our next theatre show. I know I will feel nervous again. But I also know that nervous feelings do not mean I cannot do something. My favourite part of these holidays is having time to perform, laugh and relax with my family.

SERIES 02 · WINTER CELEBRATIONS

Zara

Family time, fixing things and asking for help

📖 Read Zara's story

I am Zara, I am fourteen, and I live in Birmingham with Mum, Dad and my older brother. I enjoy technology, experiments and finding out how things work. So you might expect me to spend the winter holidays making something complicated. Sometimes I do, but not every day.

Christmas is a Christian celebration of the birth of Jesus. In Britain, lots of people also enjoy the lights, food and time off school. My British-Pakistani family does not celebrate Christmas in quite the same way as some of my school friends, and that is fine. We still have a nice family day on 25 December because we are all at home.

This year, Mum wants to cook a special lunch. I help her in the kitchen while Dad sets the table. My brother and I choose a board game for the afternoon. We both want to win, but we laugh when Dad forgets whose turn it is.

On Boxing Day, 26 December, I open a box of old wires and small electronic parts. My brother jokes that I have found the perfect activity for a day called Boxing Day. But I know the holiday is not named after cardboard boxes in our house. One explanation is that, long ago, workers received Christmas boxes with gifts or money. Some people now spend the day shopping or watching sport. I would rather fix an old desk lamp.

Unfortunately, the lamp will not work. I try several ideas. Finally, I ask my brother to help. Together, we find a loose connection, and the lamp lights up. I feel proud, but I also realise I wasted time by trying to do everything alone.

On New Year's Eve, we watch the countdown at home. On New Year's Day, 1 January, I tidy my desk and start planning my next project. My goal is simple: I want to keep solving problems, but I also want to ask for help sooner. Perhaps two people can find an answer faster than one.

SERIES 02 · WINTER CELEBRATIONS

Malik

Music at home, Boxing Day football and listening better

📖 Read Malik's story

I am Malik, I am fourteen, and I live in Manchester with Dad and my younger sister. I love music and I play bass guitar. For me, December has a special sound: Christmas songs, people laughing and my sister singing much too loudly in the living room.

A few days before Christmas, I help Dad make a playlist for our family. My sister wants every song to be cheerful and fast. I prefer a few slower songs too. We almost argue about it, but then Dad asks us to choose half the music each. That is actually a good plan.

On Christmas Day, 25 December, Dad cooks dinner. My sister helps set the table, and I wash the vegetables. Later, she asks me to play bass guitar while she sings. It is a bit silly, but we have a great time. For once, I listen to her ideas instead of telling her what to do.

On Boxing Day, 26 December, Dad likes watching football on television. There are often important football matches in Britain on that day. I sit with him for part of the game, even though I would rather make a new song. Dad tells me that the name Boxing Day probably comes from old traditions of giving boxes of gifts or money to workers, not from the sport of boxing.

After the match, I call two friends and we work on a short piece of music. We each have a different idea, so I have to listen carefully. The song sounds better when everybody gets a chance.

On New Year's Eve, we stay up for the countdown, and we cheer when it reaches midnight. New Year's Day is 1 January. I wake up late, eat breakfast with Dad and my sister, and write down one plan for the year: I want to listen before I speak. It may not sound like a big goal, but my friends and family will probably notice the difference.

SERIES 02 · WINTER CELEBRATIONS

Finn

Baking, a quiet Boxing Day and Scottish New Year

📖 Read Finn's story

I am Finn, I am thirteen, and I live in Glasgow with Mum and my older sister. Winter in Scotland can be cold and dark, but I enjoy being inside when something nice is cooking. My favourite part of the holidays is spending time with my family, especially my grandad.

A few days before Christmas, Grandad and I make shortbread. It is a Scottish biscuit with only a few ingredients, but it is easy to get wrong. I put too much flour into the first mixture. Grandad does not get annoyed. He just shows me how to fix it, and we try again.

On Christmas Day, 25 December, Mum and my sister open their presents while I help in the kitchen. Grandad comes to have lunch with us. We eat together and play a board game afterwards. My sister talks a lot during the game. I do not say much, but I usually notice when she forgets a rule.

Boxing Day is on 26 December. We do not have a busy plan. We take some shortbread to Grandad and spend the afternoon playing another game. He reminds me that Boxing Day is not named after boxing matches. It is linked to old traditions of giving gifts or money in boxes to workers and people in need.

Here in Scotland, New Year's Eve is also called Hogmanay. Some families have special parties and welcome the first visitor after midnight. That tradition is called first-footing. Not every family celebrates in exactly the same way, of course. We stay up to welcome the new year and sing Auld Lang Syne together.

On New Year's Day, 1 January, we have a late breakfast. I make some more shortbread for a family friend who is coming to visit. I do not need a huge party to enjoy the new year. For me, a warm kitchen, a good game and people I care about are more than enough.

Kai

A new skateboard, winter weather and trying again

📖 Read Kai's story

I am Kai, I am thirteen, and I live in Belfast with Mum, Dad and my younger brother. I like skateboarding, especially when I have time to practise without rushing. December is not the best month for skating because it often rains, but I still enjoy the winter holidays.

On Christmas Day, 25 December, we have breakfast together before opening our presents. My parents give me a set of new skateboard wheels. I am excited, but the skatepark is wet, so I cannot try them outside yet. My younger brother gets a small toy skateboard. We make a little ramp out of cardboard for his toy while Dad helps with Christmas lunch.

On Boxing Day, 26 December, the weather is finally dry. A friend asks me to meet him at the skatepark. I wear my helmet and take my board. He shows me a new turn. At first, I watch carefully because I do not want to fall. I try a small part of the move, stop and try again. By the end of the afternoon, I have improved a little.

Dad says Boxing Day is a public holiday in the UK and that many people spend it relaxing, shopping or watching sport. He also explains that its name may come from an old custom of giving boxes of gifts or money to workers. It has nothing to do with getting into a boxing ring.

On New Year's Eve, we watch the countdown together. My little brother wants to stay awake until midnight, but he falls asleep on the sofa. On New Year's Day, 1 January, the weather turns wet again, so I stay inside and clean my skateboard.

I think about the new turn I practised. I still cannot do it perfectly, and that is okay. My plan for the new year is to keep practising at my own pace. I do not need to copy everything my friends can do. I only need to try, learn and keep going.
